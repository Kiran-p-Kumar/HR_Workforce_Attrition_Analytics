# HR Workforce & Attrition Analytics Dashboard

**An Excel-based HR analytics dashboard analyzing 2,925 employee records to identify attrition patterns, workforce risk areas, overtime impact, job-role trends, and employee satisfaction drivers using formulas, Pivot Tables, and interactive dashboard reporting.**

## Why This Matters

Employee attrition is not just an HR metric — it directly affects workforce stability, hiring costs, productivity, and team capacity.

This project converts raw employee-level HR data into a practical workforce analytics dashboard that helps answer questions such as:

- How high is the overall employee attrition rate?
- Which departments and job roles experience the most attrition?
- Does overtime correlate with higher employee turnover?
- Which age groups have greater attrition risk?
- How does job satisfaction relate to employee attrition?
- Which employee groups should HR prioritize for retention initiatives?

The goal is to move from a raw employee database to a **management-ready HR dashboard** that supports faster workforce decisions.

## Business Impact

- Analyzed **2,925 employee records** with **492 recorded attrition cases**
- Identified an overall **16.8% employee attrition rate**
- Found that **R&D accounts for the largest number of attrition cases**, followed by Sales
- Identified **Laboratory Technicians, Sales Executives, and Research Scientists** as the highest-volume attrition job roles
- Compared attrition across **overtime status, departments, age groups, job satisfaction, business travel, and job roles**
- Built a reusable Excel reporting structure where the analysis can be refreshed as the underlying employee data changes

## Approach

| Stage | What I Did |
|---|---|
| **Data Preparation** | Cleaned and structured 2,925 employee records, standardized HR fields, and checked employee-level data quality |
| **Calculation Layer** | Used Excel formulas such as COUNTIF, COUNTIFS, SUMIFS, AVERAGEIFS, and percentage calculations for HR KPIs |
| **Analysis Layer** | Used Pivot Tables to analyze attrition by department, job role, age group, overtime, satisfaction, and other workforce dimensions |
| **Workforce Segmentation** | Created practical employee segments using age bands, satisfaction levels, performance status, distance status, and working-year groups |
| **Reporting Layer** | Converted the analysis into KPI cards, comparison charts, trend-style views, and an interactive HR dashboard |
| **Dashboard Layer** | Designed a management-focused single-page dashboard for quick workforce and attrition assessment |

## Key HR Insights

### Attrition by Department

R&D has the highest number of attrition cases, with **282 employees**, followed by Sales with **186** and HR with **24**.

This makes department-level attrition analysis important for identifying where retention efforts should be concentrated.

### Attrition by Job Role

The highest-volume attrition roles include:

1. **Laboratory Technician — 130**
2. **Sales Executive — 113**
3. **Research Scientist — 99**
4. **Sales Representative — 68**
5. **Human Resources — 24**

These roles can be further investigated using overtime, job satisfaction, compensation, tenure, and business-travel indicators.

### Attrition & Job Satisfaction

The dataset shows a clear relationship between job satisfaction and attrition:

| Job Satisfaction | Attrition Rate |
|---|---:|
| 1 — Very Low | 23.3% |
| 2 — Low | 17.3% |
| 3 — Medium | 17.2% |
| 4 — High | 12.1% |

Employees with the lowest job-satisfaction score show substantially higher attrition than employees with the highest satisfaction score.
